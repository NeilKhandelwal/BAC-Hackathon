METHOD (applies to every distance below)
- Distances are straight-line, nearest polygon edge to the reference point, computed in a local azimuthal equidistant projection centered on the point. "inside" means the point is within the polygon. Treat as +/-0.5 km (geometry generalized to ~30 m).
- "In county" is the polygon's area intersecting the TIGER county polygon.

TASK 2. TRIBAL CULTURAL RESOURCES AND CONSULTATION
======================================================================

Grant County / mid-Columbia
- Yakama Nation, ceded area (Treaty with the Yakima, June 9, 1855, Art. I; https://treaties.okstate.edu/treaties/treaty-with-the-yakima-1855-0698). The boundary runs from the Lake Chelan/Methow divide to the Columbia, "thence, crossing the Columbia on a true east course, to a point whose longitude is ... (119° 10')", "thence in a true south course to the forty-seventh (47°) parallel of latitude: thence east on said parallel to the main Palouse River", down to the Snake and Columbia, then "up the Columbia to the 'White Banks' below the Priest's Rapids". Quincy (119.85 W, 47.23 N) lies east of the Columbia, west of 119°10', and north of 47 N. By my reading of the treaty text, Quincy is INSIDE the Yakama ceded area, as is most of western and central Grant County. I did not check this against an official ceded-area map. Practical effect: the Yakama Nation is an "affected tribe" for Section 106, EO 21-02, and SEPA notice. Ceded land is not reservation land and carries no tribal land-use authority over private parcels.
- Wanapum. Grant PUD (https://www.grantpud.org/the-wanapum): "Brought together in the 1950s with the federal licensing of Priest Rapids and Wanapum dams, Grant PUD and the Wanapum Band of Native Americans committed to a long-lasting relationship". The Wanapum Heritage Center was "Completed in 2015" at 29086 WA-243, Mattawa, "next to the Wanapum ancestral village and Priest Rapids Dam". The FERC EA for P-2114 places the "Priest Rapids Band of the Wanapum ... Indian Village" inside the project boundary. Non-recognized status: unverified (see list).
- Priest Rapids Project, FERC No. 2114, licensed to Grant PUD. These are not federal dams. They are a county PUD project, and the FERC license is the federal nexus. Per a search summary of the FERC EA (page not fetched), there are two developments, Wanapum (14,680-ac reservoir, 735 MW) and Priest Rapids (7,725-ac reservoir, 675 MW), across Grant, Yakima, Kittitas, Douglas, Benton, and Chelan counties. Section 106 applies to anything needing a FERC license amendment or use of project lands. TIGER hydrography puts the river reach labeled "Columbia Riv" 10.6 km W of the Quincy reference point and the polygon labeled "Wanapum Lk" 29.0 km away. I did not compute distances to the dam sites.
- Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation. The 12 constituent bands include Moses-Columbia, Wenatchi, Entiat, Chelan, and Palus. The Moses Lake School District land acknowledgement places that district in "the historical homeland of the škwáxčənəxw people (Moses-Columbia or Sinkiuse) of the Confederated Tribes of the Colville Reservation" (https://www.mlsd161.org/land). That is a secondary source; I fetched no Colville primary source. Expect Colville to be a consulting party for Grant County.
- CTUIR (Umatilla): nearest CTUIR trust land is 152 km S. I found no source tying Grant County to CTUIR ceded lands. Relevance is unverified.

Consultation triggers (Washington)
- Section 106, NHPA. ACHP (https://www.achp.gov/protecting-historic-properties/section-106-process/introduction-section-106): it "requires federal agencies to consider the effects on historic properties of projects they carry out, assist, fund, permit, license, or approve". A privately funded campus on private land triggers 106 only through a federal nexus. Typical examples: an Army Corps Section 404 permit, a BPA interconnection, FERC action on hydro project lands, or federal funding or loan guarantees. Regulations are 36 CFR Part 800. I did not fetch the tribal-consultation section.
- Governor's Executive Order 21-02, signed April 7, 2021 (https://governor.wa.gov/sites/default/files/exe_order/eo_21-02.pdf). Agencies "shall consult with DAHP and affected tribes on the potential effects of projects on cultural resources proposed in state-funded construction or acquisition projects that will not undergo Section 106 review ... including grant or pass-through funding". It applies only where state funds are involved. A purely private data center is not covered unless state grants, loans, or pass-through funds are used. Consultation must finish "prior to the expenditure of any state funds". It superseded EO 05-05.
- DAHP. DAHP reviews archaeological predeterminations and surveys under the county processes (Clark County page). Its WISAARD database and project-review role: page not fetched.
- SEPA. Clark County requires archaeological predetermination for SEPA-reviewed applications in its higher-probability areas. SEPA statute and checklist text: not fetched.

Clark County
- Cowlitz Indian Tribe: reservation in the county, 23.2 km N of Vancouver. It is the clearest consulting party.
- Chinook Indian Nation: not federally recognized. It was recognized January 3, 2001, and the recognition was revoked in 2002. Headquarters: Bay Center, WA. A Ninth Circuit dismissal (June 2025) was followed by a petition to the US Supreme Court (September 15, 2025), which denied review per Chinook Justice (https://chinookjustice.org/our-fight-continues-after-supreme-court-setback/). Sources are secondary and advocacy pages. A non-recognized tribe has no Section 106 consulting-party right, though it can be invited.
- Yakama ceded area. Per the treaty text, the western end runs "down the Columbia River to midway between the mouths of White Salmon and Wind Rivers". Those river mouths are in Skamania and Klickitat counties, east of Clark, so Clark County is OUTSIDE the ceded area. Yakama treaty fishing interests on the lower Columbia are a separate matter that I did not research.
- Clark County archaeological review (https://clark.wa.gov/community-development/archaeological-review). The county adopts the statewide Archaeological Predictive Model by reference. Predetermination is required for SEPA-reviewed development "within a 21-100% predictive probability area ... and have a high impact of ground disturbance or are within a 41-100% predictive probability area ... and have a moderate impact", or within 1,320 ft of a registered site or 500 ft of a known site. The chapter number is unverified. Codepublishing shows 40.570 as the SEPA chapter (40.570.080 "SEPA and County Decisions") and 40.460.520 as the shoreline archaeology section. The page returned 403, so I could not confirm "40.570 Archaeological Resource Protection".

Franklin County, NY
- Saint Regis Mohawk Tribe. Its THPO operates under NHPA and NAGPRA (https://www.srmt-nsn.gov/programs/tribal-historic-preservation-office), and Section 106 consultations go to the THPO. Land claim: the Akwesasne Mohawk Land Claim Settlement involves New York State, Franklin and St. Lawrence counties, the towns of Bombay and Fort Covington, and NYPA. It provides near-immediate reservation status for 3,500 ac and a path to up to 14,000 ac in the claim areas (Hogansburg/Bombay Triangle). H.R. 2916 (119th Congress) passed the House on December 9, 2025. The Senate companion S. 3475 had a hearing on June 3, 2026. I found no evidence it has been enacted. The claim area lies NW of Malone, around 20-30 km, not at Malone.
- NY SHPO (https://parks.ny.gov/shpo/environmental-review/). Reviews run under "Section 106 of the National Historic Preservation Act and Section 14.09 of the New York State Historic Preservation Act" for projects "undertaken, funded, licensed, or approved by state or federal agencies". A campus needing state permits (for example DEC) or NYPA or ESD involvement would trigger 14.09.
- Adirondack Park Agency. It issues permits under Section 809 of the APA Act and 9 NYCRR Parts 570-588, keyed to private land-use classes (Hamlet, Moderate Intensity, Low Intensity, Rural Use, Resource Management, Industrial Use), as shown in APA permit documents (for example https://apa.ny.gov/files/permits/issued/2024/P2024-0061-Permit-Final.pdf). APA land-use jurisdiction applies only inside the Blue Line, so not at Malone.

TASK 3. ASSESSMENT
======================================================================

Grant County / Quincy: no blocking protected-land or tribal-land conflict. The nearest protected fee land is WDFW's Columbia Basin Wildlife Area, 5.8 km W, with the Quincy Lakes Unit 8.6 km S. There is no federal conservation designation (NWR, NPS unit, wilderness, national forest) within 30 km; BLM multiple-use parcels begin 7.2 km NE. There is no wilderness or NPS unit within 50 km, and no reservation or trust land within 74 km. Quincy already hosts a cluster of hyperscale data centers. Search snippets of SEC filings list Microsoft (~204 ac), Dell/NTT (80+ ac), Vantage, Oath/Yahoo, H5 (ex-Intuit), and Sabey; secondary sources add CyrusOne. Existing permitted precedent on the same land type is the strongest evidence that a 300 MW campus is permittable here. The real constraint is procedural, not locational. Quincy appears to fall inside the Yakama 1855 ceded area. Colville (Moses-Columbia) and the Wanapum have documented interests along the mid-Columbia. Any federal nexus (BPA interconnection, Corps 404, FERC P-2114 project lands) brings Section 106 consultation with these tribes. State money brings EO 21-02. Upland irrigated farmland in the Quincy industrial area is lower archaeological risk than land near the Columbia, Crab Creek, or the coulees. These are "nearby but not a conflict" for a parcel in the existing industrial zone. The binding constraints for 300 MW at Quincy are more likely power allocation from Grant PUD and transmission, which are outside this screen.

Clark County / Vancouver: no blocking conflict for a well-sited parcel, but the county has more sensitive features and they are closer. A site at the reference point itself is implausible: it is urban, and Fort Vancouver NHS is 1.2 km away. For unincorporated north or east county, avoid four things. The Ridgefield NWR lowlands (13 km NW). The Shillapoo/Vancouver Lake lowlands (4 km NW). The Columbia River Gorge NSA, which covers ~31 km2 at the far east end; NSA rules would seriously constrain an industrial campus there, so treat it as a real constraint. The Cowlitz Reservation near La Center (23 km N). Gifford Pinchot NF (~5 km2) and the DNR NAP/NRCA at Washougal Oaks are small. The Cowlitz Tribe is the primary consulting party. Clark County's code-based archaeological predetermination, keyed to the predictive model, adds a survey step for most SEPA-reviewed parcels in moderate or high probability areas. That costs time, not a veto. Clark is outside the Yakama ceded area per the treaty text. Rating: siting constraint, not a blocker.

Franklin County / Malone: no protected-land or tribal-land conflict at Malone itself. Malone is 6.1 km outside the Adirondack Blue Line, so APA land-use jurisdiction does not apply. Nearest Forest Preserve (Chazy Highlands WF) is 10.6 km SE. Nearest state forest (Deer River) is 10.3 km SW. The St. Regis Mohawk Reservation is 24.8 km NW. No NWR or NPS unit is within 50 km. Two real constraints exist at county scale. First, about 68% of the county is inside the Park. Any site south of the Blue Line needs an APA permit, and large-scale industrial use is limited to specific private land-use classes. Many such sites also sit next to Forest Preserve lands. Treat that as a serious constraint. I did not check APA's land-use map for Industrial Use or Hamlet areas in the county's park towns. Second, the Akwesasne land claim settlement is pending in Congress as of the latest evidence (House passed December 2025; Senate hearing June 2026). It could restore reservation status to land in Bombay and Fort Covington, 20-30 km NW. That matters for sites in that direction, not at Malone. The Saint Regis Mohawk THPO would be a Section 106 consulting party for any federal nexus. NY SHPO 14.09 applies to any state funding, permit, or approval. Rating at Malone: nearby but not a conflict.
